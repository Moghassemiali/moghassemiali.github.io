I am a Post-Doctoral Research Associate at the <a href="https://uwm.edu/" style="color: #1E90FF; text-decoration: none;"> University of Wisconsin-Milwaukee </a>, working with Dr. Robert Cuzner at <a href="https://sites.uwm.edu/sees/" style="color: #1E90FF; text-decoration: none;"> Center for Sustainable Electrical Energy Systems (SEES)</a>. In my current postdoctoral role, I am working on several ONR-funded projects at the interface of power electronics, shipboard systems, electro-thermal management, and digital twins. Key efforts include: **NiPEC-PEPDS Solution Space Exploration and Digital Twin Based Dielectric Scaling Law and Health Prognosis:** Developed integration framework for PEPDS, focusing on Virtual Prototyping Process (VPP) leading to metamodels of Power Electronic Power Distribution System (PEPDS) equipment within the Navy; **Tech Candidate for Future Navy Integrated Power and Energy Corridor:** Collaborated with the Power Electronic Power Distribution System (PEPDS) integration team to bring a range of technologies developed on the PEPDS; **Power Energy Thermal Technologies and Integration Systems (PETTIS):** Co-development of an equipment sizing tool with UT-Arlington for shipboard Battery Energy Storage Systems (BESS) suitable for the exploration of power electronics, battery technologies and thermal management approaches; **Demo D Preparation:** Co-development of the PEPDS hardware demonstration platform with UT-Arlington and establishes bases of cost estimates, providing validation cases and "new sources of truth" to increase the confidence in equipment metamodels and measurement data.

I received my Ph.D. in Electrical Engineering at <a href="https://www.clemson.edu/" style="color: #1E90FF; text-decoration: none;"> Clemson University </a>, advised by Dr. Christopher Edrington at <a href="https://eciclemson.wixsite.com/mysite" style="color: #1E90FF; text-decoration: none;"> Real Time COntrols and Optimization Laboratory (RT-COOL)</a>, where I was honored to receive two **Outstanding Graduate Researcher** awards from the <a href="https://www.clemson.edu/cecas/departments/ece/" style="color: #1E90FF; text-decoration: none;"> Holcombe Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering</a> (department-wide award) and also from <a href="https://www.clemson.edu/graduate/" style="color: #1E90FF; text-decoration: none;"> Graduate School</a> (university-wide award) at Clemson University. My Ph.D. research, funded by ONR, developed multi-objective electro-thermal management for modular multilevel converter (MMC)-based power electronic building block (PEBB) concept in all-electric ship (AES) systems. I integrated model predictive control, electrical–thermal characterization, and data-driven degradation forecasting to improve PEBB’s intelligent, adaptive, and resilient performance under dynamic operating conditions. These contributions were validated on HiL/CHiL platforms.

Before joining Clemson University, I received my M.Sc. and B.Sc. in Electrical Engineering from Islamic Azad University - South Tehran Branch in Tehran, Iran, in 2015 and 2012, respectively. My master's thesis focused on response and reliability improvement of grid-connected photovoltaic systems with a novel trans-Z-source inverter (Trans-ZSI) and maximum constant boost control (MCBC) method. My bachelor's thesis centered on power flow optimization of the distribution network in the presence of distributed generations (e.g., photovoltaics and wind turbines).

My research focuses on the design, modeling, and control of intelligent, advanced power electronics converters for transportation electrification, renewable energy systems, and power system applications.
